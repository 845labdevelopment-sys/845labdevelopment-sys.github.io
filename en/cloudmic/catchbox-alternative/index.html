CloudMic Live vs Catchbox

Facts checked November 2026 · prices quoted from public listings and resellers (street prices) and may change · 845.Lab

Catchbox is a soft, throwable wireless microphone: you toss it to whoever wants to speak, and it plays through the venue's PA. CloudMic Live turns the phones people already hold into microphones, with the host approving who is live from a Windows PC. One is a physical object that needs no software at all; the other is software that needs no extra object. Both avoid the "please wait for the mic" moment, in very different ways.

Which one fits your room

Catchbox is the better fit when…

  • You want a physical, playful moment — throwing the mic is part of the event.
  • There is no usable Wi-Fi at all (outdoor events, venues that forbid networks).
  • The venue already has a wireless mic system and a sound engineer.
  • Participants may not have phones, or should keep them in their pockets.
  • You need something that works with zero screens and zero setup on the day.

CloudMic Live is the better fit when…

  • The room has Wi-Fi and everyone has a phone.
  • You want a queue — people request, the host decides the order — instead of whoever catches the box.
  • You also want polls, a question box or a projector view from the same PC.
  • Budget is per event rather than a purchase: a day pass is a few dollars.
  • Nothing physical should be passed around (classrooms, formal meetings, hygiene rules).

What it costs

Catchbox: the vendor's site listed kits from about US$1,149 in November 2026; UK resellers listed some models at roughly £440–560. Prices depend on the kit (whether a transmitter and receiver are included) and the region. Please confirm with Catchbox or a reseller.

CloudMic Live: free for up to 5 participants with all core features. For bigger rooms, an event pass is US$4.99 for 24 hours, or US$9.99 a month / US$69.99 a year (adds recording, exports and reports). Participants are always free. Purchases go through Microsoft Store.

In one sentence

Catchbox makes the microphone fun to pass; CloudMic Live makes passing it unnecessary. Choose by whether your room has Wi-Fi, and whether you want the host, or the catch, to decide who speaks.

Common questions

Can I use both?

Yes. Some hosts use a throwable mic for the energetic part of an event and CloudMic Live for the orderly Q&A, since the PC output can feed the same PA.

How does CloudMic Live sound compared with a real microphone?

It uses the phone's microphone and the room's speakers, so quality depends on the phone and the speakers. It is built for speech in rooms, not for singing or broadcast.

What if the venue Wi-Fi blocks phones from reaching the PC?

Turn on the PC's Mobile hotspot and let phones join that instead. No internet connection is required.
