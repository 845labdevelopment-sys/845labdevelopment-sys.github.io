Features

Run the room
without passing a mic.

Spot the raised hand, carry the mic over, get it back. CloudMic Live removes that loop by bringing every request to the host's screen.

01

Phones become microphones

Participants scan a QR code and open a web page. While they are speaking, their voice plays from the host's PC.

02

Three speaking modes

Request & approve, free push-to-talk, or host nomination. Switch to fit the moment.

03

The host stays in control

Per-person mute, global mute, lock, remove and ban. You can also invite someone to speak, right from the PC.

04

Buzzer and reactions

The buzzer records who pressed first, automatically. Applause and laughter reactions add up on the host's screen.

05

Random pick

Pick one participant at random. For calling on students or drawing a name at an event.

06

Entirely on the local Wi‑Fi

No internet needed. Audio is played live and never sent to the developer or any outside server. An event passcode and join lock keep the room to the people you invited.

07

Polls and a question box

Ask a question with options and let people answer on their phones. Shy participants can ask in writing; the most liked questions rise to the top.

08

Projector screen and speaking timer

A separate screen view shows the current speaker, time remaining, poll results, buzzer ranking and the join QR code, large enough for the room.

09

Announcements, roll call and audio care

Send a break notice to every phone. Roll call is one tap. Level meters show each voice, and the app warns about possible feedback.

10

Word cloud and private notes

Collect short words on a prompt and show the popular ones larger on the screen. Participants can also send the host a private note such as "The sound is too quiet".

11

Visible turns, fair picks

People waiting to speak see "You are number 3 in line". Random pick can favor those who have not spoken yet. Applause and cue sounds play through the room speakers.

12

Roster, poster and venue check

Match attendance against a CSV roster. Print a QR poster for the door. Before the event, measure in 10 seconds how many people this PC and Wi-Fi can handle. English and Japanese interface.

How it works

Three steps to get started.

1

Create an event on your PC

Launch the app on Windows and create an event. Make sure the PC is on the same Wi‑Fi as your participants.

2

Participants scan the QR code

Show the QR code on screen. Participants scan it with their phone — no install, it opens in the browser.

3

Approve, and they're on

A participant taps "Request to speak". When the host allows it, their voice plays from the PC.

Privacy

Voices stay in the room.

Audio and event data travel only between the host's PC and the participants' devices on the local network. Nothing is sent to the developer or to third-party services. Audio is normally played live and not stored; with a paid plan's recording feature it is saved only on the host's PC. The app contains no analytics or advertising SDKs.

FAQ

Questions

Does everyone need to be on the same Wi‑Fi?

Yes. The host PC and the participants' phones must be on the same Wi‑Fi (or the same LAN). There is no way to join remotely over the internet — which also means no voice or event data ever passes through an outside server.

If the venue's Wi‑Fi blocks devices from talking to each other, you can connect phones to the PC's Mobile hotspot instead.

A security warning appears on the phone the first time it connects

Because CloudMic Live uses the microphone, the host PC serves the page over HTTPS with a certificate it generates itself. That certificate is not issued by a public authority, so the browser warns once on first access. The connection never leaves your local network, so it is safe to proceed.

Android (Chrome)

  1. On "Your connection is not private", tap Advanced
  2. Tap Proceed to (address) (unsafe)

iPhone (Safari)

  1. On "This Connection Is Not Private", tap Show Details
  2. Tap visit this website, then Visit Website to confirm

If it still won't connect, the "?" button on the host screen explains how to use an alternative address.

Can I run a Teams or Zoom meeting and a live room at the same time?

Yes. In the host app, "Output" lets you play the room's voices on the venue speakers and send them to the meeting app at the same time. The meeting app receives the audio through a free virtual audio cable (BlackHole on Mac, VB-CABLE on Windows): select that cable as the microphone in Teams or Zoom. The steps are shown from the "?" next to "Output" in the app.

Remote attendees cannot speak through their phones; only people on the same Wi‑Fi can join.

Is audio recorded?

Normally, no. Only when a host on the monthly or yearly plan uses the recording feature is audio saved, as a file on the host's own PC. While recording, every participant's screen shows a "Recording" indicator. Audio is never sent to the developer or any server.

The microphone is used only between "Start speaking" and "Stop speaking".

Do participants need to install an app?

No. They open the host's QR code in Safari (iPhone) or Chrome (Android). No account is needed, and participants are always free.

How is this different from Slido, Mentimeter, Teams or Zoom?

Text Q&A tools collect written questions for the host to read out; participants never speak. With CloudMic Live, a participant's phone becomes the microphone, so people talk from their seats — and polls and a question box are included.

Teams and Zoom deliberately mute extra microphones in the same room to prevent howling, so they are not designed to pick up questions from the floor. CloudMic Live plays the room's voices on the venue speakers and, if you like, sends them to Teams or Zoom at the same time.

What is CloudMic Live not built for?

A few things, and we would rather say so up front.

  1. 1,000-seat halls. It is designed for meeting rooms, classrooms and lecture rooms. A consumer Wi‑Fi router or a PC hotspot supports a limited number of phones reliably.
  2. HDMI output to venue displays or pro audio connectors. The screen view uses the host PC's display output and the audio uses the PC's speaker output.
  3. Remote attendees. Only people on the same Wi‑Fi can join; online attendees cannot speak through their phones.

For large events that need these, a dedicated receiver-based system is the better fit.

What does the host app run on?

Windows 10 (version 1809 or later) and Windows 11. It is a free download from Microsoft Store.
